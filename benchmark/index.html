Managers stop at the chat. Nobody shows them the next step.

BAIN-B01 · Method

How we made the benchmark

Before the numbers: who answered, what we asked them and how to read the results.

Who answered

Experienced managers who work in product, project and delivery, business analysis and operations. 781 people from 26 countries answered. 746 of them answered all questions.

The first group came from LinkedIn and answered our online form. Fieldwork: October to November 2026. No payment and no prize. We do not publish names or company names.

What we asked

Each person answered the same 10 questions in one online form, in about 3 minutes. The questions are about:

  • The role, the years in the role and the size of the company.
  • How they use AI at work, and which task they would give to AI first.
  • How they keep up with AI, and if their company supports them with AI.
  • Their biggest pain with AI at work.
  • How much time they would put in to learn, to get 5 hours back each week.

How to read the numbers

  • A percentage is the share of the people who answered the question. Each number gives its base.
  • Some questions let a person select more than one answer. Then the shares of the answers can add up to more than 100%.
  • The ladder step is the highest step that a person uses.
  • Each free-text role is in one of 5 role groups.
  • With 746 people, a result of 50% can be up to about 4 points higher or lower.
  • People who reply to a message about AI are more interested in AI than the average manager. The benchmark describes this panel, not all managers.

BAIN-B03 · The ladder

Five steps from chat to agents

On a higher step, the AI has more context about the work and does a larger part of it.

How we put a manager on a step

  • The ladder comes from one question of the form: "How do you use AI at work?". Each step is one answer to this question.
  • A manager can select more than one answer. We put the manager on the highest step that they selected. For example, a manager who selected chat and an agent connected to work tools is on step 4.
  • Each manager is on one step only. As a result, the five shares add up to 100%.
  • Each step is a different tool. A chat in the browser does not see your work tools. An AI agent is connected to work tools, for example Jira, Slack and email.
  • The average step is the mean of the steps of all managers, from 0 to 4.

BAIN-B05 · Findings

9 findings

Each number is the share of one group of managers. Each finding gives its base.

Finding 01 · Use

55% stop at the chat

55% of the managers do not go past the chat. Only 33% connect an AI agent to their work tools, or build own automations. Base 746.

Finding 02 · Use

Experience does not move them

Of the managers with 10 or more years in the role, 57% stop at the chat. Experience in the role does not teach a new way to work. Base 137.

Finding 03 · Training

86% got no training

86% got no AI training from their company: tools but no training 37%, on their own 40%, freelance or between roles 6%, AI is restricted 2%. Base 746.

Finding 04 · Training

Large companies do not train either

Of the managers in companies with 5,000 people or more, 84% got no AI training from their company. Base 207.

Finding 05 · Trust

70% do not trust the output

This is the most frequent pain. When you do not trust the output, you check all of it again, and AI does not save time. Base 746.

Finding 06 · Trust

Tool training does not fix trust

Only 14% get time, tools and training from their company. Of these managers, 66% still do not trust the output. Training on a tool does not teach a way to check the result. Base 104.

Finding 07 · Learning

They learn alone

53% learn AI by trying new tools themselves. Only 21% learn from courses. Base 746.

Finding 08 · Time

47% give it less than 5 hours

To get 5 hours back each week, 47% would put in less than 5 hours in total, or not now. 30% say that they have no time to learn. Base 746.

Finding 09 · Time

Past the chat, managers give it more time

Of the managers on steps 2 to 4, 63% would put in 5 hours or more to learn. Of all managers, 53%. Base 335.

BAIN-B08 · Our reading

What this means for a manager

This part is our opinion, not data. We train managers to work AI-native, so read it with this in mind.

The gap is the method, not the tool

Almost all managers in the panel have AI. Most of them use it as a chat: they ask, copy and paste. A new tool does not change this. A new way of working does.

Change how you think about the work

AI-native work starts in the head. Give the AI the context of your work. Cut the work into steps that AI can do. Make checking part of each step. Then trust grows with use.

Learn alone, or with help

Most companies do not teach this yet. You can learn it yourself: take one task that you do every week and change how you do it. Or learn with people who already work this way. Then you get there faster.
